# candidate-outreach

**When to use:** A recruiter asked for outreach or follow-up text for one candidate (email or LinkedIn), or asked whether contact is appropriate, and a human will review and send.

**When not to use:** A reminder to follow up with no draft ask; auto-send expected; bulk sequences across a list; a second version beside an existing draft.

## Purpose

Produce one initial message and one follow-up for a specific candidate and role, plus short judgment notes. Pick the best angle and commit. Assistive only: drafts stop at awaiting human send.

## Inputs

- **Candidate:** profile or resume text, or a candidate-assessment read. Profile URLs often cannot be fetched; work from pasted text.
- **Role:** hiring company, team, location and on-site days, scope, and what the role offers that the candidate's current role does not. Reuse a role-requirements profile when one exists.
- **Employment model:** in-house, agency direct-hire, agency contract, or other. It sets the identity line. If missing, ask once.
- **Operator context:** relationship, comp room, anything the candidate has said before.
- **Channel:** email, LinkedIn connection note, or LinkedIn message (only after the connection is accepted).
- Optional: an existing draft to tighten.

## Outputs

- A one-line verdict when it matters (for example, do not contact).
- Exactly one **initial** draft and one **follow-up**.
- 3-5 notes: the lever used, facts to verify before send, likely objections.
- The completed review checklist; status **awaiting human send**.

## Procedure

1. Confirm an explicit draft ask (or "should we contact?"). A reminder to ping is not permission to write.
2. **Pre-flight.** If one fails, say so and do not draft:
   - **Eligibility.** If the person works for the hiring company now, or the brief or profile marks them ineligible, stop and say so. Full rules live in role-requirements.
   - **Personal connection.** Never invent how you found them; never reference private information. If they raise a connection, acknowledge it.
   - **Role facts.** If the role plainly does not fit (location, level, field), flag that outreach is pointless.
3. **Read the candidate:** 2-3 specific hooks (projects, results, moves, not adjectives); an archetype; risks to handle implicitly (overqualification, relocation). Never mention gaps or short tenures as judgment.

| Archetype | Signals | Primary lever | Comp line? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active seeker | Open to work, recent grad, recent layoff | Role fit and specifics | No |
| Contractor near the end of an engagement | Contract title, fixed-term dates, a year or more in | What comes next: a longer horizon or a permanent seat | Light hint |
| Growing mid-career | 2-5 years in, scope rising | The career move: the scope or step they cannot get where they are | Light hint |
| Senior passive | Long tenure, senior title | Ownership and mission | Only if the operator can beat total comp |

4. If an existing draft is on the table, tighten it. Do not fork.
5. Write the **initial** (60-120 words for email):
   - **Subject:** `<Role> at <Company> (<Location>)`.
   - **Identity, one line:** who the operator is and how the role is employed, stated plainly for the employment model. The candidate should never have to ask "is this a contract?"
   - **Hook, then the career move:** why this role is a step for this person, tied to the hook.
   - **Role facts:** scope and location up front.
   - **One ask,** conditioned on the real filter.
6. Shape by channel: email is one screen with one ask; a connection note is 3-4 sentences with shared ground and no pitch; a LinkedIn message comes only after the connection is accepted.
7. Write the **follow-up** (about a week later) that adds something new: a new fact, an explicit "not now is fine" out, or the job description for active seekers.
8. **Job description:** leave it out of the initial by default. Share it in the follow-up for active seekers, or whenever asked. Check that the public posting matches the message.
9. Run the checklist, then hand off. Do not schedule or send. If pressed to auto-send, refuse.

## Hard rules

- No comp figures, rates or contract terms. Hints only, per archetype.
- No calendar links in cold outreach.
- No filler openers, hype or exclamation points.
- Do not invent role facts; mark unconfirmed ones verify-before-send.
- One ask per message.

## Review checklist

- Every factual claim appears in the brief.
- The employment model is clear from the identity line.
- No invented results, interviews or rapport.
- One clear ask; the channel voice matches.
- Status set to awaiting human send.

## Worked synthetic example

**Brief (synthetic):** Dana Whitfield, Account Executive at Copperline Payroll (fictional), read as screen first by candidate-assessment. Role: mid-market AE at Fernvale (fictional), agency direct-hire through Ridgeway Talent (fictional), hybrid in Denver. Archetype: growing mid-career. Channel: email.

**Verdict:** clear to contact.

**Initial:**
Subject: Mid-market Account Executive at Fernvale (Denver)

Hi Dana, I'm a recruiter at Ridgeway Talent, helping Fernvale hire a mid-market Account Executive. This is a full-time role on Fernvale's own payroll, not a contract. Your work selling payroll software to finance and HR leads at Copperline is close to what this seat needs, with bigger deals and longer cycles: the AE owns each deal from first call to signature. The team is small, and the role is hybrid, two days a week in Denver. If a move into larger deals is on your list this year, would you be open to a 20-minute call?

**Follow-up:**
Hi Dana, one detail I left out: Fernvale plans a second mid-market team next year, and the first AEs will help shape its territories (verify before send). If the timing is wrong, a quick "not now" is completely fine.

**Notes:** lever is the step to larger deals; verify the second-team plan; likely objection is a commission-heavy plan.

## Failure modes / edge cases

- A reminder mistaken for a draft ask; an ineligible candidate drafted anyway.
- An identity line that leaves the employment model unclear, so the first call is spent on "is this a contract?"
- Invented rapport, results or role facts.
- A job description in the initial, causing a silent self-opt-out.
- A pitch in a connection note, or a pitch on a bare accepted connection.

## Forever don'ts

- Never auto-send; drafts stop at awaiting human send.
- Never draft unasked; never fork parallel versions.
- Never invent experience, results, interviews or rapport.
- Never hard-code one employment model or one vendor's pitch.
- No real candidate PII or client names in shared examples.
